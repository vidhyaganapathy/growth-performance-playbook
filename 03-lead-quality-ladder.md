# 03 · Lead-quality ladder

CPL tells you how *cheap* leads are, not how *good* they are. Climb the ladder as far as your data allows.

```
5. Pipeline / revenue          ← what the business cares about
4. Cost per meeting booked
3. Cost per qualified lead     ← the best everyday metric
2. Lead-to-qualified rate
1. CPL                         ← where most reporting stops
```

## Defining "qualified"

Agree a definition with sales or the founder **before** reporting on it. For example:

| Criteria | Example |
|---|---|
| Fit | Right company size, industry, location |
| Role | Decision-maker or influencer |
| Intent | Responded, or has a clear need or timeline |
| Not | Students, job seekers, competitors, existing customers |

## Getting the data without owning the CRM

1. Every lead carries its **source, campaign and ad** (UTMs, form fields).
2. Sales marks each lead **qualified / not qualified / meeting booked**, in the CRM or a simple shared sheet.
3. Once a week or month, match outcomes back to campaigns.
4. Where possible, send qualified leads back to the ad platforms as conversions, so they optimise for quality.

## How it changes decisions

| Campaign | CPL | Qualified rate | Cost per qualified lead | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | $40 | 10% | $400 | Cheap, poor quality |
| B | $120 | 50% | $240 | Expensive, better value |

*Illustrative numbers only.* On CPL alone, Campaign A looks better. On cost per qualified lead, B is the better investment.

## For consumer apps

Use the same ladder with app events:
**Cost per install → cost per signup → cost per activated user → retention**
